## The problem

Reporting misconduct is hard, and harder still in a second language. Reports arrive vague or missing the details an investigator needs, and asking for them is delicate. Meanwhile, the same kind of conduct can keep falling through gaps in company policy without anyone noticing a pattern.

ReportIQ helps the person reporting write something actionable, and helps the organisation see what its policy doesn't cover. It was built and tested against a code of conduct modelled on a large manufacturer's.

## Three layers, and where the AI stops

The split is deliberate. The model is good at reading messy writing, so it reads. Deciding what an investigator needs and how to ask a frightened person for it is left to rules people can review.

## Checking against policy and law

Each account is matched against two bodies of text at once: the company's own policy and the EU Whistleblower Directive. Retrieval combines meaning-based search with keyword search, then re-ranks the results.<Note>Dense embeddings and BM25 fused with reciprocal rank fusion, then an LLM re-rank. See <code>backend/app/rag/</code>.</Note> The result says whether the conduct is covered by policy, by law, by both or by neither, and every quote it shows is verified character for character against the original.

## Closing the gaps

Conduct that neither policy covers is grouped as it builds up. Once enough similar reports arrive close together, ReportIQ proposes an amendment attached to a specific existing clause. A person accepts, edits or rejects it. Nothing changes policy on its own.
